11.1 Introduction
In this lesson we are going to learn about the singular points of a differential equation and
nature of power series solutions near different types of singular points.

11.2 Objectives of the lesson
By the end of this lesson you should be able to:
   •  define ordinary points and the singular points of a differential equation.
   •   classify the singular points into Regular Singular points and Irregular Singular
       points of the differential equation.
   •   write the nature of power series solution which may exist at an Ordinary point,
       Regular Singular points and Irregular singular points of the given differential
       equations.

11.3Power series solutions
We have learnt that many types of differential equations have solutions that can be

        expressed as finite linear combinations of known elementary functions.

       However, many equations may not have solutions that can be found in the

        usual methods we have studied so for.  In such case we must seek other

       means of expression for the solutions of such equations. One such method

         of obtaining solutions is infinite series form. The solution so obtained is

         called as Power Series Solution of the given differential equation.


Definition of power series solutions.
All  differential equations either  first order or higher order need not possess

         solutions.  Under certain conditions some equations may have solutions

       which can be expressed as an infinite series of the form.
    y = C0 + C1 (x – x0) + C2 (x – x0)2 + ………+ Cn (x – x0)n +…… to ∞ or
          ∞
    y =        C n ( x − x0) n                                                             (5.1)    ∑
              n = 0
     where C0, C1, C2………..are constants
     Solutions of the form (5.1) are called Power Series Solutions of the differential
      equations.

In order to confirm whether an equation does have solution of a power series or not

      we must know certain basic definitions that will be seen now

11.4Definition of Analytic Function at a point
   A function f (x) is said to be analytic at x0 if its Taylor series about x0
       ∞   f n ( x0 )           n                    ( x − x0 )  exists and converges to f (x) for all x in some open interval  ∑        n = 0  n!
     including x0.

     Sometimes we say that any function that can be differentiable at the point x0 is
      called an analytic function at x0
      Alternatively we can say that f (x) is analytic at the point x0 if the function does not
    become infinite when x = x0.

11.6Definition of an ordinary point and singular point of the differential equation
    The point x0 is called an ordinary point of the equation (5.3) if both of the functions
     p1 and p2 are analytic at x0.
       If either p1 or p2 or both of these functions is not analytic at x0 then x0 is called a

     singular point of the normalized equation (5.3).

11.8 Existence Theorem and the nature of Power Series solutions
     Consider the equation (normalized form)
    d 2 y     dy
       + p1   + p 2 y = 0                                                  (1)           2     dx      dx

     I.  If the point x0 is an ordinary point of the equation (1), it has two nontrivial

       solutions of the form

          ∞
    y =        C n ( x − x0) n    ∑
              n = 0

    II.  If the point x0 is a regular singular point of the equation (1) it has at least one

       nontrivial solution of the form.

                  ∞
            C n ( x − x0 ) n    y = x − x0 r ∑
                        n = 0
           ∞
        C n ( x − x0) n + r where r is a constant (real or complex). This     or y = ∑
              n = 0
     solution is valid in some deleted interval 0 < x − x0 < R where R>0 about x0.


  III.  If the point x0 is an irregular singular point, we are not assured about the

       existence of solution of the equation. It may or may not have the power

        series solution near the irregular point at x0.

    Hence before we assume a power series solution at x0 for the normalized
     equation we must examine the nature of the point x0 and proceed.
